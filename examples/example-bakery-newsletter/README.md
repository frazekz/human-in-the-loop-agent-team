# Example: Example Bakery newsletter (fictional)

**Everything in this folder is invented.** Example Bakery does not exist; there are no real people, addresses, prices, or customers here.

## The situation

A small neighbourhood bakery wants a short monthly email newsletter for the customers who signed up in the shop. The owner has no marketing team and about one hour a month for this.

## What happened, step by step

| Step | Role | What happened | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Coordinator | Filled the brief from a one-line goal; asked the owner two questions (tone, consent) | [brief.md](brief.md) |
| 1 | Coordinator | Planned three steps, each with a check | [status.md](status.md) |
| 2 | Executor | Wrote draft v1, with assumptions declared | [outputs/newsletter-draft-v1.md](outputs/newsletter-draft-v1.md) |
| 3 | Reviewer | Returned 3 fixes: an invented statistic, a missing consent check, an untested link | [outputs/review-v1.md](outputs/review-v1.md) |
| 4 | Executor | Wrote draft v2 with the fixes | [outputs/newsletter-draft-v2.md](outputs/newsletter-draft-v2.md) |
| 5 | Reviewer | `clean` | [log.md](log.md) |
| 6 | Coordinator | Reported to the owner and asked for a yes. **Nothing was sent.** | [status.md](status.md) |

## What to notice

- **The coordinator never wrote the newsletter.** It only kept the brief, status, decisions, and log.
- **The reviewer caught an invented number** ("30% of our customers…"). It had no source, so it was removed.
- **Consent is an assumption until the owner confirms it.** The coordinator asked instead of assuming.
- **Approval of the draft is not approval to send.** The final step is a question with options.
